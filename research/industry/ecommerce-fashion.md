# E-Commerce and Fashion Retail

## Business context

- **Typical goals:** Support product discovery, communicate brand and fit, reduce purchase uncertainty, increase conversion and order value, retain customers, and manage collection launches.
- **Audiences:** New shoppers, returning customers, gift buyers, style-led browsers, price-sensitive buyers, and social referrals.
- **Critical user questions:** Is this for me? How does it fit? What does it look like on a person? What material and care? Is my size available? What are shipping and returns? Is the brand credible?
- **Trust barriers:** Weak product imagery, unclear sizing, hidden shipping/returns, poor variant controls, generic descriptions, fake urgency, and template-like merchandising.
- **Typical journey:** Discover collection or product → browse/filter → inspect imagery and fit → evaluate price, delivery, returns, and trust → select variant → add to cart → checkout.
- **Primary conversions:** Add to cart and complete purchase; secondary conversions include email/SMS signup, wishlist, store visit, and collection discovery.

## Brand personality

Product and audience dependent: editorial, lifestyle, playful, premium, heritage, streetwear, or utility. Shopping clarity must survive the art direction.

## Visual language

Collection-aware art direction, strong on-body and detail imagery, clear product hierarchy, useful filters, visible variants, fit and material information, and purposeful merchandising.

### Effective conventions

- Put decision-critical information before promotional filler.
- Use authentic category-specific media.
- Make the primary action visually dominant without repeating it mechanically.
- Adapt density, typography, and component behavior to audience urgency and information complexity.

### Overused conventions and clichés

Full-screen autoplay fashion video, illegible thin serif, excessive whitespace hiding products, generic sale bars, floating quick-add everywhere, and identical Shopify-theme sections.

### Differentiation opportunities

Build around product silhouette, customer identity, fabrication, styling system, collection narrative, local store experience, or founder point of view.

## Information architecture

- **Recommended navigation:** New, Shop/Categories, Collections, Best Sellers, About/Story, Journal/Lookbook, Search, Account, Cart; utility for shipping, returns, sizing, and store location.
- **Core pages:** Home, Store, Category/Collection, Product, Search, Cart, Checkout platform, About, Lookbook, Size Guide, Shipping/Returns, FAQ, Contact, Store Location.
- **Internal linking:** Link high-intent pages to proof, process, relevant FAQs, locations, and the next conversion step. Avoid isolated campaign or blog pages.
- **Structured data:** Product, Offer, ProductGroup, Organization, BreadcrumbList, shipping and return policy markup where supported.


## Page-pattern rules

### Homepage
- **Intent:** Confirm fit, orient users, and expose the highest-value pathways.
- **Hierarchy:** Context-specific proposition → primary route → proof → relevant choices → next action.
- **Avoid:** A universal hero/features/testimonials/CTA formula.
- **Mobile:** Prioritize the dominant task and reduce competing promotional content.
- **SEO:** Brand plus core category context; link to authoritative deeper pages.

### Detail page
Use the relevant detail type: program, service, product, project, donation, event, or practitioner.
- Answer decision-critical questions before decorative storytelling.
- Place proof near claims.
- Make price, schedule, location, eligibility, variants, scope, or next step visible when verified.
- Use structured data matching visible content.
- On mobile, keep the selected item and primary action contextually clear.

### About/team page
Explain why this specific organization is credible. Include real history, governance, philosophy, team credentials, and community context where verified. Avoid a generic values-card grid.

### Contact/location page
Show verified contact methods, hours, accessibility information, service area, arrival expectations, and response timing. Use a short purpose-driven form.

### Resource/FAQ page
Organize by user task and link answers to authoritative detail pages. Do not use FAQ content to conceal missing core-page information.


## Conversion strategy

- Define one dominant conversion per page.
- Use a secondary action only when it serves a distinct lower-commitment journey.
- Match form length to commitment and qualification needs.
- Explain what happens after submission.
- Place trust evidence at the point of doubt.
- Do not manufacture urgency or social proof.

## Example-analysis protocol

For every example used in a project, capture:

1. Audience and top task
2. Primary and secondary conversion
3. Page composition and reading order
4. Type, color, media, shape, density, and motion system
5. Navigation and information scent
6. Trust evidence and claim proximity
7. Mobile adaptations
8. Accessibility and performance risks
9. SEO and structured content
10. Transferable principle
11. Identifiable composition or stylistic element that must not be copied

## Industry-specific automatic failures

- Required decision details hidden or omitted
- Synthetic or stock media presented as real participants, staff, projects, or products
- Unverified trust claims
- A generic visual personality that could be transferred unchanged to an unrelated industry
- Primary conversion obstructed on mobile
