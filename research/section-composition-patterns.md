# Section Composition Strategies

Use this vocabulary while planning page rhythm. These are composition strategies, not templates to rotate randomly. Select or adapt a strategy because its hierarchy serves the content semantics, user intent, available media, conversion role, and selected art direction.

Multiple implementations of one archetype should still differ when their content relationships differ. This list is not exhaustive; create a new composition when the content calls for one, and name its governing idea in the composition map.

| Strategy | Hierarchy source and appropriate use | Mobile composition idea |
|---|---|---|
| Editorial split | A large statement in one region and narrative content offset beside it; useful when a thesis and explanation need simultaneous weight. The offset and resulting negative space need a stated compositional role. | Preserve the statement's scale, then place the narrative with deliberate indentation, rules, or spacing rather than centering both or retaining desktop dead space. |
| Image-led | Photography or illustration establishes context before text; useful when authentic visual evidence can answer the user's first question. | Re-crop to retain the subject, keep the decisive text attached to the image, and avoid shrinking the image into a thumbnail. |
| Statement | One oversized phrase or sentence with minimal support; useful for a defining promise, belief, or transition. | Maintain typographic dominance and shorten the measure; reduce scale only enough to prevent broken reading. |
| Asymmetric split | Unequal columns or intentionally offset elements express priority or tension; column starts and centers of gravity must still read as one composition. | Reorder by meaning and preserve asymmetry through scale, inset, overlap, or spacing without breaking semantic or focus order or delaying primary content. |
| Feature grid | Comparable items are arranged for scanning or selection. Use only when the items are genuinely parallel objects. | Change column count or use a labelled horizontal scroll when comparison remains clear; do not turn every item into an oversized stacked card. |
| Metric-led | Verified statistics or numbers establish proof and hierarchy. | Use a compact stacked band, ordered list, or two-column numeric rhythm while keeping labels attached to values. |
| Quote-led | A verified testimonial, founder statement, athlete quote, or customer voice is the primary visual element. | Preserve quotation scale and attribution proximity; treat portraits as supporting evidence, not decoration. |
| Timeline | Chronology establishes structure and helps users understand change over time. | Convert horizontal time to a vertical progression with explicit dates, current position, and logical reading order. |
| Process | Sequential steps establish the structure for a task, service, or pathway. | Use a numbered progression with clear step boundaries; disclosures are acceptable when every label remains understandable. |
| Showcase | A portfolio work, product, project, property, event, service example, or case study dominates visually. | Preserve the featured object's prominence, crop intentionally, and keep its caption or action connected. |
| Gallery | Visual media communicates more than introductory copy. | Use a purposeful swipe or scroll sequence with position cues, captions where needed, and keyboard-accessible controls. |
| Bento / modular | Differently sized modules express different levels of importance. | Reorder by priority and retain varied emphasis; avoid flattening every module into identical cards. |
| Horizontal narrative | Content progresses laterally when a desktop sequence benefits from comparison, chronology, or spatial storytelling. | Become a vertical progression or accessible labelled scroll while preserving sequence and emphasis. |
| Immersive | A full-width or full-height visual environment carries atmosphere or evidence with restrained copy. | Keep a meaningful crop and readable contrast; reduce height when necessary without separating copy from context. |
| Minimal | Whitespace and a small amount of content create focus without decorative hierarchy. | Preserve breathing room proportionally and keep the single purpose obvious rather than filling the space with extra copy. |
| Comparison | Options, services, tiers, approaches, or choices are shown in relation to one another. | Prioritize key differences, use a scroll region or disclosure where necessary, and repeat labels so context is never lost. |
| Conversion | The offer and action dominate with reduced supporting content. | Keep the action early and reachable, preserve essential reassurance, and avoid a generic detached CTA band when context belongs nearby. |
| Logo / trust proof | Verified partners, certifications, clients, publications, or affiliations establish credibility. | Use a compact proof strip or labelled grid with readable marks and alternative text; do not imply unverified relationships. |
| Long-form narrative | Paragraphs, imagery, pull quotes, captions, and supporting details form an editorial reading rhythm. | Maintain readable measure and vary media placement without disrupting source order or creating excessive scrolling fragments. |

## Selection test

For each major section, identify:

1. The user or business purpose and key message.
2. The content type with the strongest truthful evidence.
3. The element that should dominate and why.
4. The hierarchy source: heading, image, product, statistic, quote, sequence, comparison, schedule, action, or another content type.
5. How the section changes the page's scale, density, alignment, whitespace, or media behavior relative to its neighbors.
6. How the same design idea survives on mobile.
7. For prominent type, the intended measure and whether any manual line break is essential to the art direction.
8. For large negative space or offset columns, what the space frames, separates, emphasizes, or contributes to rhythm.

If two neighboring sections use the same hierarchy, alignment, silhouette, and spacing cadence, either document why the repetition communicates a relationship or change the weaker composition.

## Consistency without sameness

Keep the brand coherent through typography, color, spacing tokens, interaction behavior, imagery treatment, iconography, and voice. Use composition, scale, density, alignment, section height, media placement, whitespace, hierarchy, interaction, and sequence to create content-driven rhythm.

Component reuse and visual consistency are not the same as repeating the same composition. Reuse tokens and primitives aggressively. Reuse a major section composition only when the content relationship genuinely calls for the same structure. A design system should constrain visual language without forcing every page into the same sentence structure.
